The story in 60 seconds

The token
$ASIMOV is a meme token on Solana, launched on StonkFun and paired against NVDAx. It has no intrinsic value and no roadmap of moons.
The pair
NVDAx is a tokenized NVIDIA share issued by Backed Finance, backed 1:1 by shares held by a custodian. $ASIMOV is not an NVIDIA share and isn’t backed by one. NVDAx isn’t available to US persons.
The author
Isaac Asimov’s “Liar!” (1941) contains the first known use of “robotics.” “Runaround” (1942) set out the Three Laws. His robot stories were collected as I, Robot in 1950.
The cat
In I, Robot (2004), the witness at Dr. Lanning’s house is a tabby-and-white cat with a red collar. The film never says its name; the trivia files say Asimov. Isaac Asimov loved cats too, and co-edited Magicats! (1984) and Magicats II (1991).
The Nexus Bots
R5 (pearl white, green visor) and R7 (metallic green, glowing circuit lines) are fictional robots from our animated series ASIMOV.
The real robot
Asimov 1 is Menlo Research’s open-source humanoid: 1.2 m, 35 kg, 25 actuated joints, released under CERN-OHL-S-2.0. We show it in our teardown. Menlo isn’t affiliated with us and hasn’t endorsed this.

Asimov facts

The word “robotics” first appears in a 1941 Isaac Asimov story, “Liar!” He wrote the Three Laws a year later, for robots that didn’t exist yet.

Isaac Asimov wrote three laws to keep robots from hurting people. We wrote seven to keep holders from hurting themselves.

Before you buy the token, consider buying the book. Start with I, Robot (1950).

Foundation follows Hari Seldon, whose psychohistory predicts the fall of a galactic empire. Worth your weekend more than any chart.

Cats of science fiction

In I, Robot (2004), the only witness is a tabby-and-white cat. The film never names it. The trivia files say Asimov.

Isaac Asimov co-edited two cat anthologies: Magicats! and Magicats II. The robot guy was a cat guy.

Heinlein’s cat Pixie searched every door in the house for one that led away from the snow. It became The Door into Summer.

Ray Bradbury on creativity: “You treat ideas like cats: you make them follow you.”

Terry Pratchett’s cat Patch slept in a desk drawer while he wrote. Science fiction runs on cats.
